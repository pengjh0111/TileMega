## 1. Synchronization note (§0.4 summary)

Four pipelines reach the GPU. Dense decode: the repository's own reference decoder -> `torch.export`
-> `BuildModelPlan` (consumer-side RMS, gamma folded into the packed weights, shared layer scratch,
per-layer K/V views of one `[L][2][B][Hkv][Cap][D]` allocation) -> CTG -> the native solver (rule-pruned
space, fixed per-stage formulas over measured constants, predicted top-4, then measured P-SEL) -> schema-5
tmexec coordinates -> `emitNativeDenseProgram` -> nvcc sm_90a. The device runs one persistent grid of 132r
CTAs x 160 threads: four consumer warps on mma.sync, one producer warp issuing bulk weights and TMA
activations into an mbarrier ring, a V3 grid barrier (or D8 group counters after qkv) between stages, one
launch per request. MoE decode shares that path with six stages per layer (section 5); its experts keep the
legacy prefill's 64x64 pages and its prefill is R1's frozen binary. TP needs a device dimension in
ModelPlan (per-rank shapes, placement, shard recipes), CTG/tmexec (a cross-device reduction edge), the
solver (per-rank workload, t_ar, ar_protocol), the bodies (an all-reduce epilogue, a cross-rank argmax),
the runtime ABI (symmetric buffers, `tm_plan_create_tp`) and the harness (comm library, TP engine,
multi-card lock).
F1-F22 hold at `8bb96de0`; nine wording differences are recorded in `runs/sm90r4/sync/onboarding.md`
without redesign, on the R3 precedent.

## 3. Phase A0: the two new models

`configs/models/{mistral_7b_v0_3,olmoe_1b_7b}.json` pin the revisions `caa1feb0` and `6d84c485` and
the config SHA256s, with `dimensions` from `model_registry.dimensions()`. Both match appendix A,
including OLMoE's `norm_topk_prob = false` and its expert width of 1024 from `intermediate_size`
(there is no `moe_intermediate_size`); `rms_norm_eps` is absent from its config and `OlmoeConfig`
defaults it to 1e-5, which the registry records. The registry admits the `mistral` and `olmo`
tokenizer families and checks architectures. Prompts are frozen to 64 tokens per model from the same
Gutenberg passages.

**T3 `SM90R4_baselines_new.csv`**: three fresh processes per cell under the R16 protocol, every
process rebuilding the name-seeded checkpoint and all recording the same `checkpoint_sha256`
(`c853abd1df...`), which is the evidence that the engines and TileMega read identical weights.
Fastest-engine TPOT in ms, B1 to B16: Mistral 6.046 (SGLang), 6.108 (TRT), 6.212 (TRT), 6.554 (SGL),
6.850 (SGL); OLMoE 1.654, 2.262, 3.376, 4.273, 5.306, all SGLang.

Two engines refuse OLMoE, recorded as N/A with their own errors (§1.7): TRT-LLM 0.21.0's PyTorch
backend raises `Unknown architecture for AutoModelForCausalLM: OlmoeForCausalLM`, and torch.compile
on transformers 4.57.1 raises `_prepare_4d_causal_attention_mask_with_cache_position() missing 1
required positional argument: 'device'`. Mistral's torch.compile baseline needed one harness fix:
`MistralConfig` stores `head_dim = null` rather than omitting it, which `StaticCache` cannot consume.

## 4. Phase A1: Mistral-7B-v0.3

The reference decoder covers Mistral unchanged (`--model mistral` routes to the llama modules) and
the pinned config has `sliding_window = null` and no `rope_scaling`. The exported decode graph
differs from Llama-3.1-8B's only in the two vocabulary shapes (32768 vs 128256), the structural
evidence that no body or packing change was needed; all five prefills are built from the R2 closeout
options of the matching Llama-8B cell. A B1 development cell solved by prediction gives TPOT
**5.323 ms** with C-1 over 256 positions (max gap 0.047) and C-2 on both executors, i.e. 1.136x the
fastest baseline before any measured selection. The five accepted cells are absent: their first
P-SEL exposed a solver legality gap and the re-solve at the merged HEAD had not finished.

**Solver legality fix.** Mistral's lm_head is 32768 wide, so TN 256 yields 128 tasks for a 132-CTA
grid, and D9 has every CTA publish one argmax partial into the head's `[B][tasks]` outputs;
`tm_plan_create` rejected such a plan with `native grid exceeds the lm_head partial outputs`. The
space now requires one lm_head task per CTA, in `Legal()` and in the candidate list.

## 5. Phase A2: the MoE corrections

### 5.1 MR0 and DT-M0 (verified)

Stage-ledger clones of the REF-R3 B1/B16 binaries (`TILEMEGA_TRACE_STAGE`, past 567-582, the
untraced reference timed in the same process) cost +6.7% and +0.47%, so neither is a timing artifact.
The measured step exceeds the solver's prediction by 1494 us at B1 (4677 vs 3183) and 3124 at B16
(17404 vs 14280). Shares of that gap, qkv and attention together since D8 blurs their boundary:

| cell | experts | router | qkv+attention | o | lm_head |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | 320 us (21%) | 406 us (27%) | 648 us (43%) | 118 us (8%) | 2 us |
| B16 | 1598 us (51%) | 826 us (26%) | 511 us (16%) | 185 us (6%) | 7 us |

Every type falls under MC1-MC7 and none outside them exceeds 15%, so DT-M0 took the "continue"
branch. One difference from the prompt's expectation is recorded: at B1 the expert stages are 21% of
the gap, below the 40% the table anticipated, because the router's last-arriver tail (7.7 us per
layer, against the 3.9 us standalone constant) and the attention merge dominate there instead.
T7 `SM90R4_moe_ledger.csv` carries the per-type rows.

### 5.2 What was implemented

Each item is a separate commit, with the unit tests named in the prompt:

- **MR1** (`cb5b4265`): each expert task reads its schedule record once into registers (expert and
  count in every lane as one broadcast transaction rather than lane 0 alone, which avoids a shuffle;
  lane r < 16 reads row r), prefetches the next local task's record one task ahead, computes one
  weight base per task, reads n_active beside the first record, and gathers rows through
  `__shfl_sync`. **Item 6 passes on the SASS**: in REF-R3's gate_up producer the generic schedule
  loads sit between the empty wait and the copy issues (indices 58-504 after the wait at 48) and
  down reads one per slot; in MR1 they are hoisted above the wait (128-132, wait at 170) and nothing
  but `LDGSTS` gathers and `UBLKCP` weight copies follows it. Generic loads in the whole gate_up
  producer fall 24 -> 3.
- **MR2** (`5b03ffdd`): the expert families take the largest slot count in [2, 8] whose
  `SharedBytes - 1024` fits the arena (3 slots for gate_up, 5 for down at these coordinates, against
  R3's 2 and 3); the dense families keep the D5 rule.
- **MR3** (`bd670b71`): the router's last arriver selects the top-k from packed
  `(ord16(bf16) << 16) | (0xFFFF - e)` keys with one `__reduce_max_sync` per round, a warp's tokens
  interleaved and the logits read once up front, with both softmax modes (`selected` for Qwen3,
  `all` for OLMoE) and E generalized to {64, 128}. A new unit test compares 160 random cases with
  forced ties and signed zeros against an FP64 reference over both modes and three batches: 0 errors.
- **MR4** (`768b5b0e`): the Q+1 rotations are spread over D/8-lane groups with every global read of
  the task issued first, and both merges read all S x Q log2-sum-exps once, derive each query's
  maximum and total, and accumulate one float4 per thread per round in batches of 16 partials. The
  attention test covers the FP64 grid with poisoned rows, S = 1, the widest merges and -- newly --
  a DSMEM merge checked bit-for-bit against the last-arriver merge; B1 and B16 pass over five fresh
  processes each, B2/B4/B8 over one.
- **MR5** (`9603c1fd`): the attention space offers every power-of-two chunk count down to 1.
- **MR6** (`cb142092`, `f7ed4d7a`): `BW_cta_dram`, the last-wave rate `min(BW_cta_dram, BW_dram/r)`,
  `n_active = E(1 - (1 - k/E)^B)` from the workload, and `t_task_expert` derived by subtracting a
  task's slot bytes at `BW_sm` and its measured epilogue from a two-point per-task slope.

### 5.3 Constants after MR3 and MR4 (verified, five fresh guarded processes)

`BW_cta_dram` = **145.8 GB/s** for one CTA with 8 x 16 KB in flight, against `BW_dram`/G = 24.6
GB/s per CTA when all 132 are active: a stage's last, partly occupied wave runs about 6x faster per
CTA than the fair share, the effect MR6 prices. `BW_dram` 3241.4 and `BW_sm` 65.27 match R3.

`t_route_la` (the probe's metric: the router's 16 tasks, the last arriver's top-k, routes and
schedule, then the V3 barrier) improves to **3.30 us at B1** (R3: 3.872, -15%) and **7.26 us at B16**
(R3: 11.616, -38%); the OLMoE form (E = 64, softmax over all) measures 6.98 us at B16. **MR3's
targets of 1.5 and 2.5 us are not met.** The metric includes the V3 barrier (768 ns at G132) and the
16-task stage, so the last arriver's own cost is about 2.5 us at B1 and 6.5 us at B16. The design is
implemented as specified -- `REDUX` appears in the probe's SASS -- so the shortfall is in the
target, not the mechanism. The remaining attention, epilogue, gate and expert-task sections were
still measuring when the machine was taken (section 10), so T8 is not in this report.

MR4's own constants were previewed on a free card and exposed a deviation in my first implementation:
it recomputed each query's maximum and total inside every thread's output loop, one `exp2` per output
element where the prompt specifies S x Q for the whole merge. Measured `t_merge(d128, s16)` in ns:
R3 5065.7 (q8) and 2534.3 (q2); the first MR4 version 5455.6 and 2899.2, i.e. **slower than R3 on
both**; with the weights normalized once but one thread per query 5018.2 and 3426.3 (q2 worse, since
one thread serializes 16 chunks); with one warp per query, which is what `43cd105b` commits, 4812.6
and 2640.4. The committed form is 5% below R3 at q8 and 4% above it at q2. `t_qprep(q8)` is 1045 ns
against R3's 1766 (-41%). **MR4's targets (<= 2000 and <= 1500 ns for the merges, <= 700 ns for the
preparation) are not met.** The arms already built for the MR4 and DT-M2 pairs carry the pre-fix
merge and must be rebuilt before either decision is taken.

### 5.4 MR1's own pair: a regression, unresolved (B1)

REF-R3 against the MR1 arm at R3 coordinates, GPU0, five fresh processes per side, A/B alternating,
trial concurrency: the reference medians 4.4200 ms (samples 4.302/4.420/4.280/4.652/4.462, range
8.42%) and the MR1 arm 4.7877 ms (4.790/4.673/4.794/4.787/4.788, range 2.54%). The MR1 arm is
**8.32% slower** and was slower in every pairing, yet the threshold rule reads it as
indistinguishable because the reference arm's own range inflates the threshold to 16.84%. R3's
recorded P-FINAL for this cell is 4.674 ms, between the two arms, so the reference arm also ran
faster here than its own final number.

That window overlapped two other MoE pair chains and another user's job, the condition R3 measured
at 4-15% variance, so neither branch is taken on it: the cell is re-queued with every other job of
mine stopped, a stage-ledger clone of the MR1 arm is prepared, and §1.3's stop condition is decided
on that measurement. MR1's mechanism is present (section 5.2), so if the regression holds the cause
lies elsewhere in the change -- the by-value record spilling in the producer and the shuffle in the
gather loop are the candidates the ledger separates.

### 5.5 DT-M1 (partial)

B1, on a shared card with `NUMA_CONCURRENCY_TRIAL=1`: the D5 arm medians 4.4828 ms and the deep-ring
arm 4.6093 ms, so the deep ring is 2.82% slower while the threshold is 8.78%, which the rule reads as
**indistinguishable**. The threshold is inflated entirely by the D5 arm's 4.39% process range

re-measured with the machine serial before DT-M1 is decided; B16 had not finished. The deep ring is
in the solver but no MoE cell has yet been accepted with it.

## 6. Phase A3: OLMoE (export verified, body partly built)

The reference decoder now covers OLMoE and **matches HF to 3.87e-07 in fp32** (max absolute logit
difference at scale 0.82) on a two-layer, eight-expert shrink of the pinned config with HF's weights
copied in. That check establishes the three semantic differences at once: the q/k RMSNorm applies to
the whole projected row before the heads are split, the router takes an fp32 softmax over all experts
and keeps the selected values without renormalizing (cast to the activation dtype), and the expert
op's own gate/up split is a plain concatenation. Both phases are exported, bridged and packed into
`configs/serving/sm90r4/inputs/olmoe_1b_7b/`; the bridge needs `--pytree-module tilemega.moe.export`
so the custom op is registered, otherwise `torch.export.load` masks the failure as a format error.

Two device-side pieces are in: the MoE region matcher accepts an unnormalized top-k (`154c9484`), and
the attention body gained Q = 1 and a `row` norm mode reducing the token's whole q (Hkv Q D) and k
(Hkv D) row across its Hkv groups, the gamma slice following the element's position in the row
(`01c329af`); its test sweeps Hkv 16, D 128, Q 1 against an FP64 reference and passes at B1/B4/B8.
Not built: the ModelPlan path for E = 64 with the softmax mode, the expert shapes and per-TN packing,
the prompt mode, the solver's OLMoE candidates, the generalized routing check. No OLMoE cell is timed.
