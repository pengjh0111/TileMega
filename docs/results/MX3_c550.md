## 2. Primitives (MX3-1 item 4)

19 probes in [`MX3_primitives_c550.csv`](MX3_primitives_c550.csv), each timed
in-kernel against the 100 MHz realtime counter (**verified**). Against the
calibrated BF16 profile:

| primitive | measured | calibrated | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| dependent read chain, 256 MiB | **363.3 ns** | 345.4 | 1.05 |
| dependent read chain, 8 MiB | 318.4 ns | 277.9 | 1.15 |
| atomicAdd dependent, 104 CTA same address | **230.2 ns** | 215.0 | 1.07 |
| `__syncthreads`, 128 and 256 threads | 14.7 ns | 27.0 | 0.55 |
| V3 grid barrier, 104 CTA | **471.5 ns** | — | — |
| V3 grid barrier, 208 CTA | **678.4 ns** | — | — |
| `__shfl_xor` | 53.3 ns | — | F6's 46 ns |
| `__nanosleep(0)` | **534.3 ns** | — | — |

**F11 is refuted.** DRAM latency is ~363 ns, not the ~1.06 µs inferred from
Little's law on P-10, and the calibration already carried 345 ns. The chain
does not separate a 64 KiB working set (442 ns) from 256 MiB, so it bounds
latency rather than resolving the hierarchy; either way 1.06 µs is not
supported, and "C550 latency is ~3x H800" with it.

Independent `atomicAdd` measures 9.9 ns, 22x under the calibrated 215 ns, but
independent atomics pipeline; the dependent form, which is what a handoff
pays, is 230 ns and agrees to 7%. The field is named
`atomic_uncontended_ns` while its value matches the *contended dependent*
case. Grid barrier at 208 CTAs is 44% dearer than at 104 — the cost side of
residency 2 (MX3-4 item 1), now measured.

## 3. GEMM mainloop pipeline (MX3-2)

`ServingGemmMma<TileM,TileN,TileK>` had no `Stages` parameter: the outer loop
was `#pragma unroll 1` and issued a block's loads, consumed them, then moved
on, while `TaskPriceParts.cpp` divided each iteration's latency by `stages-1`
*and* dropped `stages-1` iterations. 27 of 149 MX-2 tile selections claimed
depth 8 or 10 (**verified**), all on `16x32x64`, where the body's only overlap
was `kUnroll=4` within an iteration.

**Does a pipeline take effect here?** No MetaX disassembler ships with this
install (`/opt/maca/mxgpu_llvm/bin` has `llvm-dis` and `llvm-nm`, no
`llvm-objdump`; `cucc -device-bin` emits an ELF whose machine id decodes as
Synopsys ARCv2.3, which system `objdump` refuses; `-S`, `--save-temps` and
`-device-bc` produce nothing), so the spec's disassembly check is unavailable
and the question is answered behaviourally — a pipeline collapsed by the
compiler's own wait-for-all could not respond to depth at all
([`MX3_pipeline_probe_c550.csv`](MX3_pipeline_probe_c550.csv),
`experiments/mx3/pipeline_probe.cu`, two runs within 1%, **verified**):

| variant | depth 1 | best | speedup |
|---|---|---|---|
| stream, no compute | **195 ns/load** | 221 ns at depth 6 | **0.89x, slower** |
| with 2 MMA per load | **755 ns/load** | **226 ns at depth 6** | **3.34x** |

195 ns/load is 1.088 TB/s, 68% of peak, so the no-compute loop has nothing
left to hide and every depth costs. With MMA occupying the pipe the serial
form is latency-bound and depth 6 recovers 3.34x, within 16% of that
bandwidth bound; the monotonic response is the evidence that the explicit
`arrive_gvmcnt` works. A shared-memory ring was slower at every depth, so the
implementation is the register form. Two implementation facts, both
**verified**: the ring slot must be a compile-time constant, since indexing
the register array with `chunk % kDepth` put the pipeline in local memory and
cost 5.8x the serial form (the chunk loop is an `index_sequence` fold); and
per-CTA regions must be disjoint, an earlier probe having walked 104 CTAs over
one stream 1 KiB apart, which stayed L2-resident and read 3.05 TB/s, above
DRAM peak — which is how the error showed.

MMA order is unchanged, so K accumulation is bit-identical and C-2 still
holds. `test_mx_task_bodies.py` passes 5/5, the tiled GEMM covering every
legal depth (1, 2, 3, 4, 6) across 5 tiles, 4 shapes and split-K
(**verified**); the test bodies are now parameterised by `Stages`, having only
ever covered depth 1.

### Model and body, six places (§0.3)

Depth is bounded by the register footprint (`ServingGemmDepthFits`) rather
than `smem/(2k(m+n))`, which described staged operands the body never had
(`OperatorClasses.h:103`); depth 1 is legal instead of excluded by
`stages>=2` (`BackendCostQuery.h`) and is the A/B baseline; the latency
discount is `/depth` rather than `/(stages-1)`, which divided by zero at depth
1 (`TaskPriceParts.cpp:146`); two guards that threw or returned early below
depth 2 now price it (`TaskPriceParts.cpp:113`, `TaskModel.cpp:24`); and
`Stages` reaches the body at all. The last three surfaced from a measurement,
not a reading: the pinned depth-1 build failed with `no admitted flow
candidate` — admitted to the domain, then unpriceable. Register-derived depth
limits are `16x32` to 20, `16x128` to 8, `64x128` to 5, `128x256` to 2.

### G-PIPE is not met

Qwen3-0.6B B1 decode, one geometry, only `stages` changed (**verified**, 2
rounds each, within 0.07% between rounds): depth 1 gives **5.958 ms** TPOT and
15.9 ms TTFT, depth 4 gives **5.987 ms** and 16.0 ms. +0.5% is inside the 1%
resolvable band, so on this cell the depth sweep does **not change TPOT**,
which is what the gate asks for. Depth 6 and the other development cells were
still measuring when the round ended. The inner loop gains 3.34x and the step
does not move, consistent with MX-2's finding that `floor_ms` equals
`binding_path_ns` — a schedule bound. Deciding this needs the stage ledger
(§7.1), which is why the spec puts the ledger first and why running it second
was the wrong order.

### The depth discount is unanchored

`--task-bodies serving` fits each body as
`fixed_ns + byte_ns*bytes + flop_ns*flops` with **no depth term** (24 entries
in `configs/targets/xcore1000.json`), measured with `Stages=2` hardwired
(`lib/Target/ServingTaskCalibration.cu:83-85`, **stated**), while the cost
model applies `/depth` analytically. Re-running the segment would refresh the
depth-2 numbers but could not anchor depth; the calibrator has to sweep it
first. The segment was **not** re-run. For scale, `gemm_store`'s fitted
`fixed_ns` is 8582 ns, and 112 such tasks would be 0.96 ms of a 6.05 ms step.

## 4. Decode attention through MMA (MX3-3)

`PrefillAttentionMma` already carries the whole decode contract
(**verified** by reading and by test): RoPE with q-norm (`:50`) and k-norm
(`:87`), the KV cache write by the owning block (`:90`, `:96`), per-block
partial and LSE, and a direct context write when `cmax == 1` (`:111-117`).
mma16 is therefore a selection, not a new body, through a
`std::conditional_t` on `TILEMEGA_ATTENTION_IMPL`, with the `IMPL==3`
assertion at `IndependentAttentionTaskBody.h:19` removed. `test_decode` passes
for both implementations across 5 geometries and 4 (batch, past, block)
settings at the same tolerance, and `test_mx_task_bodies.py` is 5/5
(**verified**).

A quantified constraint (**verified**): mma16 stages Q, K, V and the
probabilities in shared memory, needing
`ServingAttentionSharedBytes(D, KvTile)` = 11.5–38.5 KiB against the SIMT
body's ~2 KiB. D=128 with a 64-token KV tile is 38.5 KiB, over the 32 KiB a
second resident CTA leaves, so **mma16 and residency 2 are in tension**
there; `compile.cpp` rejects it with a diagnostic rather than a
`static_assert` from inside the generated translation unit.

One fault was mine: the first test wiring reused the prefill kernel, which
pins `c=0` and sweeps the query block, the opposite of decode's mapping, so
position 700 was never written and the new K row was wrong by 4.48. A
decode-shaped kernel (`qb=0`, one task per KV block) fixed it; the body was
never at fault.

**G-ATTN is not met**: C-1/C-2 were not run on the development cells, the
simt-against-mma16 A/B was not measured, and mma16 is a CLI flag rather than a
searched coordinate, so the solver cannot select it.

## 6. Where MX-2's review was contradicted

- **F11**: DRAM latency ~363 ns, not ~1.06 µs (§2), so C550 is not ~3x H800
  on latency and the appendix-A reasoning that rests on it needs redoing.
- **MX-2's mcTlass verdict** was wrong in the way the review says: 736 µs for
  6.3 MB is 0.5% of DRAM bandwidth, a configuration fault, and comparing one
  `gate_up` call against the mean of 112 heterogeneous invocations was not
  like-for-like. The code was not in git either. MX3-7 was not reached, so no
  replacement measurement exists.
- **F1/F2 confirmed** exactly as written, including the double discount.

## 7. Unfinished, with causes

1. **The stage ledger (MX3-1 items 1, 2, 3, 5) is the largest gap.**
   `MX3_stage_ledger_c550.csv` does not exist. The analyzer is written
   (`scripts/mx3_stage_ledger.py`: bound, inefficiency, imbalance, startup,
   gaps, epilogue, barrier, residual reported not distributed) and the runtime
   already allocates and dumps `stage_trace.tsv`, `task_trace.tsv` and
   `step_trace.tsv` (`ServingRuntime.cuh:530`, `:751`) behind
   `tilemega.serving.trace`. Those traces are compile-time macros with no
   flag, so `compile.cpp` gained a validated `TILEMEGA_TRACE` hook taking only
   STAGE, TASK and STEP. A traced TM-MX2 plan was still building when the
   round ended, from a worktree at `e9e4e5ae0` carrying only that hook so the
   device code stays TM-MX2. Instrumentation overhead is unmeasured,
   `MX3_resources_c550.csv` was not produced, which body pushes registers to
   248 is unknown, and counting local accesses needs the absent disassembler.
2. **MX3-2**: the task-body calibration segment was not re-run, and cannot
   anchor depth until the calibrator sweeps it (§3); same-stage prefetch
   (item 3) was not tried; the shared-memory ring was measured only in the
   probe, never built into the body.
3. **MX3-3**: no C-1/C-2, no end-to-end A/B, not selectable by the solver, and
   the transposed S = K·Q^T mapping of item 2 was not explored — the 16-row
   query tile leaves QPerKV=2 using 2 of 16 rows.
4. **MX3-4, MX3-5, MX3-6 and MX3-7 were not started.** MX3-6, the 20-cell
   final measurement, is on the spec's do-not-cut list, so this round is not
   complete on its own terms.
5. **G-REPRO** was never exercised from a clean clone, only in-tree.
6. `--evaluate-configs` cannot pin `Ec`/`Rq` (§1), so no pinned rebuild
   reproduces a plan exactly.
