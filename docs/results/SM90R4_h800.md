## 2. Synchronization note (§0.4 summary, §10.2)

1. Dense decode is unchanged from R3: the repository's reference decoder, `torch.export`,
   `BuildModelPlan` (consumer-side RMS, gamma folded into the packed weights, per-layer K/V views of
   one `[L][2][B][Hkv][Cap][D]` allocation), CTG, the native solver, schema-5 tmexec coordinates,
   `emitNativeDenseProgram`, nvcc sm_90a.
2. The solver is a rule-pruned space, fixed per-stage cost formulas over measured constants, the
   predicted top-4, then a measured P-SEL; R4 adds an `expert_fuse` dimension at schema 6.
3. The device runs one persistent grid of 132r CTAs x 160 threads: four consumer warps on mma.sync,
   one producer warp filling an mbarrier ring, a V3 grid barrier (or D8 group counters after qkv)
   between stages, one launch per request.
4. MoE decode shares that path. R3 had six stages per layer; MR7 fuses gate_up and down into one
   M56 stage behind per-slot `gu_done` counters, making five.
5. Qwen3-30B-A3B keeps the legacy prefill's 64x64 expert pages; OLMoE needed per-TN packing
   (`Page = 0`), which `ServingPages.cpp` now sizes from the recipe's `expert_stack` node.
6. Mistral needed no body or packing change: its exported decode graph differs from
   Llama-3.1-8B's only in the two vocabulary shapes.
7. OLMoE needed five plumbing changes (section 6), one solver legality fix and a prompt mode that
   teacher-forces the prefill through the decode plan, since no separate prefill plan was built.
8. Measurement is unchanged: P-PAIR (A/B alternating, threshold `max(0.5%, 2x worst spread)`),
   P-SEL (A B C D D C B A), P-FINAL (fresh processes, median of medians), P-CORR (C-1 vs HF, C-2
   executor token equality, HF64), all behind the per-card flock guard and device-identity checks.
9. TP would need a device dimension in ModelPlan, CTG/tmexec, the solver, the bodies (an all-reduce
   epilogue and a cross-rank argmax), the runtime ABI and the harness. None of it exists.
10. F1-F22 hold at `8bb96de0`; nine wording differences are in `runs/sm90r4/sync/onboarding.md`
    without redesign, on the R3 precedent.

### 5.4 MR7: the fused stage, and a real synchronization bug it exposed

The first fused cell hung end to end, about once per 25k fused stages, and 200 unit-test launches
never met it. Per-thread barrier markers in a host-mapped instrumented clone localized it: in every
stuck CTA all 128 consumer threads had **arrived at** the stage's final `__syncthreads` and all 32
producer lanes had **passed** it. `__syncthreads` is `bar.sync 0`, the `.aligned` form, counted per
warp execution, so a producer warp that reached it split into separately scheduled groups completed
the 160-thread barrier alone, left the stage, and left the consumers on the next barrier generation
forever. The divergence was MR7's own: only lane 0 ran the data-dependent `gu_done` spin.

The fix (`60ccedb93`) has every producer lane poll `gu_done` (a broadcast load), lane 0 then issue
the `h` copies, and an explicit `__syncwarp()` reconverge the warp. Verified twice: the instrumented
clone, which stalled within nine decode steps on every earlier run, completed 6 x 1023 steps
(294,624 fused stages) clean; and the production B1 cell -- the same cell that twice hit the guard's
5400 s timeout -- passes P-CORR with C-1, C-2 over five fresh processes and HF64. A new unit test
(`test/unit/sm90_dense_moe_fused_test.cu`) checks the fused stage against an FP64 reference under
random per-CTA arrival skew, at both packings. Eight rejected hypotheses are in the decision log.

The re-solve then **selected the fused stage** at Qwen3 B2/B4/B8/B16 and OLMoE B1/B2, so the
mechanism is both correct and competitive on predicted cost; **its end-to-end gain is unmeasured**,
because the finals it would be judged on are not comparable (next paragraph).

### 5.5 The Qwen3-30B-A3B re-test: measured, but not publishable

After the user relaxed §1.5, the re-solved fused cells were measured beside other jobs. They are
slower than R3's machine-serial P-FINALs by +0.22% (B2), +8.60% (B4) and +11.07% (B8) -- inside the

## 6. Phase A3: OLMoE-1B-7B -- built, correct against HF, not reproducible (§10.4)

`configs/models/olmoe_1b_7b.json` pins revision `6d84c485` and matches appendix A, including
`norm_topk_prob = false` and the expert width of 1024 from `intermediate_size`. The reference
decoder **matches HF to 3.87e-07 in fp32** on a two-layer, eight-expert shrink with HF's weights
copied in, establishing the three semantic differences at once: the q/k RMSNorm applies to the whole
projected row before the heads are split, the router takes an fp32 softmax over all experts and
keeps the selected values unnormalized, and the expert op's gate/up split is a plain concatenation.

Both phases export, bridge and pack; all five cells solve. Five plumbing gaps were found by the
builds themselves and fixed: the whole-row q/k norm was silently dropped by the solver's attention
key (`8bc6de66f`, `15b86caf6`); `ModelPlan` admitted only E = 128 (`8e4b8e0c1`); `ServingPages.cpp`
read the expert count from a `dm_access` node that native plans do not emit, and its dense-consumer
guard rejected the `moe_gate_up`/`moe_down` epilogues (`c5d6cb1bb`, `c2fe578bf`); and
`rotary_tables()` had no `OlmoeForCausalLM` branch (`f061dbdce`). A 16-head, one-query attention body
and a `row` norm mode were added with their own FP64 test, and the solver gained OLMoE's 64-expert
and one-query candidates with their measured constants.

**The open defect.** Every OLMoE cell fails C-2: the same binary in the same configuration does not
reproduce its own output. Quantified on one card, one binary, three 64-token runs: two runs of the
identical configuration differ in **1 token of 64** (first at step 4), and across launch paths 11 of
64. At the first divergence two of the three runs agree and the reference is the outlier -- the
signature of a near-tied argmax flipping on a ULP, not of corruption; over 1024 tokens C-2 is then
certain to fail, as it does on all five cells. Qwen3-30B-A3B is bit-identical over 1024 tokens on
four cells, so the instability is on OLMoE's own path, not in the shared MoE or fused-stage code.

**C-1, run alone so the HF comparison could run at all, PASSES** on the B1 cell: 256 positions, max
logit gap 0.031, 98.8% of positions bit-equal in the greedy token, `pass: true`. That bounds the
defect: the kernel agrees with HF, and what is wrong is one unstable reduction, not the arithmetic.
Four hypotheses were tested and rejected by experiment (empty attention chunks in prompt mode -- the
qwen3_0_6b prompt-mode control passes C-2 with 16 chunks; the fused stage at `Page = 0` -- the unit
test now takes a `TEST_PAGE` knob and passes there over two seeds x 20 launches; the row norm's
reduction -- a shuffle tree plus a fixed four-term shared sum, no atomics; and iteration bookkeeping
across `launch`/`launch_steps`). The narrowest remaining suspects are the `SoftmaxAll` router finish
and prompt mode itself. Per §1.3 this is reported, not worked around.

Consequently **no OLMoE cell is accepted and no OLMoE TPOT is published as a result.** T1 carries B2
(2.2030 ms) and B4 (3.3627 ms) marked **`PROVISIONAL (C-2 fails)`**; for scale they would be 1.027x
and 1.004x SGLang's 2.2622 and 3.3762 ms. Those two binaries are not the one C-1 ran on.

## 7. Phase B: MPK (§10.5)

MPK 63af900 ran **no model at all** on this host at the start of the round. Three explicit errors
were fixed with the user's authorization, captured as
`scripts/mpk_patches/mpk-63af900-sm90.patch` with the originals kept under `runs/sm90r4/`:

1. `src/kernel/task_register.cc` ~1509 (paged attention, non-split KV): the format string had 14 `$`
   placeholders for 15 annotated arguments -- `PAGE_STRIDE_ROWS` had none -- so the transpiler
   aborted on **every** sm90 `paged_attention_layer`. Fix: one more `$`; recounted 15/15/15.
2. `src/kernel/task_register.cc` ~4560 (split-KV): 14 placeholders and 14 arguments, but
   `ROTARY_DIM` was never passed, so the page stride filled the `ROTARY_DIM` slot and
   `PAGE_STRIDE_ROWS` defaulted to 0 -- the illegal memory access seen on that path. The task has no
   `rotary_dim` parameter, so the fix passes `head_dim`, the template's own default.
3. `demo/llama3/demo.py`: paged attention was called with `q_norm=None`/`k_norm=None`, which the
   current `persistent_kernel.py` rejects; per its own assertion message each layer now attaches a
   `head_dim` dummy and passes `enable_qk_norm=False`.

**Protocol differences, every one recorded in the CSV.** Output length is compiled into the kernel
(T_N needs one binary per N); with `max_num_batched_tokens = B` the prefill is chunked and admitted
staggered, so a B > 1 span is not 1023 iterations; the timing scope is one `mpk()` call; there is no
chat template; EOS is disabled; the demo's page settings are kept (16 pages x 4096). Two further
caveats that make the ratios **indicative, not controlled**: the MPK rounds were measured beside
other jobs while the TileMega cells they are compared with are R3's machine-serial P-FINALs
(contention inflates MPK and so inflates TileMega's apparent lead -- the 0.01-0.19% process spreads
suggest the effect is small here, but it is not controlled), and no MPK cell was re-measured with
the matching TileMega cell in the same window. Every row is therefore `comparable=conditions
differ`. Multi-GPU MPK (§5.4) was not run.

Three models are **N/A**, each for its own documented reason: `mistral_7b_v0_3` and `olmoe_1b_7b`
raise `ValueError: Unknown builder` -- MPK has no demo for those architectures, and §1.7 forbids
adapting an engine to a model; `qwen3_30b_a3b` compiles its megakernel (21,333 tasks) and then dies
with `CUDA error at test.cu:1060: out of memory` at layer 33 of 48, because MPK's generated host code
`cudaMalloc`s a fused qkv buffer per layer while keeping q, k and v, so the 61 GB expert stack, the
7 GB KV pool and the growing per-layer duplicates exceed the 80 GB card. That is an architectural
memory strategy, not an explicit error of the kind the user authorized patching.

`llama3_1_8b` is **incomplete**: one round of three at B1/B2/B4 and none at B8/B16, so nothing is
published for it. The cause is the demo's KV budget, and the arithmetic is unambiguous -- 32 layers
x 8 kv heads x 128 dims x 2 x 2 B = 128 KB of KV per token, and 16 pages x 4096 = 65536 tokens per
slot = 8.00 GB per slot, so with 16 GB of weights B8 needs exactly 80 GB and B16 needs 144 GB. (I
twice attributed these OOMs to another user's job; that was wrong. torch reported a 78.74 GiB
process that could not have been separate, since 78.74 + 26.95 exceeds the 79.18 GiB card -- it was
this process seen by its host pid, which differs from the namespace pid.) The R16 protocol needs
only 1088 KV tokens, so one page would cover it and the default over-provisions by 60x, but
dropping to one page changes the setting under which the four completed models were measured: the
one-page run and its llama3_2_1b control were still in flight when collection stopped and were
deleted rather than published, so **the trade-off is unresolved** and the 16-page data stands as
measured.
